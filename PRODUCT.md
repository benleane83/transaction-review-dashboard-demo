# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Financial crime operations reviewers and compliance leaders use this dashboard to evaluate flagged transactions, understand why items entered human review, and prioritize work by risk, exposure, SLA posture, corridor, transaction type, and assigned team. The working context is controlled, accountable review rather than automated decisioning.

## Product Purpose

This product is a synthetic transaction review dashboard for triaging suspicious or policy-flagged payments. It exists to demonstrate how reviewers can move from portfolio-level risk signals to transaction-level rationale, recommended next action, and reviewer notes without implying automated case outcomes. Success looks like faster human orientation, clearer escalation paths, and trustworthy evidence for every flagged item.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, accountable. The interface should feel operationally mature and confidence-building: serious enough for financial crime workflows, clear enough for executive demonstration, and restrained enough to keep attention on human judgment and risk evidence.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic fintech and SaaS-dashboard cliches: navy-and-gold prestige styling, decorative glassy KPI cards, gradient accents, ornamental metrics, and over-polished command-center drama. Avoid any visual treatment that implies automated adjudication, gamifies compliance, or makes risk status depend on color alone.

## Design Principles

- Human review is the center of gravity: every screen should reinforce reviewer ownership and explainability.
- Evidence before ornament: risk reasons, SLA posture, and recommended actions must be easier to parse than decorative chrome.
- Calm urgency: critical states need unmistakable priority without panic or theatrical styling.
- Demo-ready honesty: synthetic data and governance boundaries should stay visible without overwhelming the workflow.
- Trust through consistency: controls, statuses, and details should share one coherent product vocabulary.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Prioritize readable contrast, clear focus states, keyboard-operable controls, and status cues that do not rely on color alone. Reduced-motion preferences should be respected for future interaction and animation work.
