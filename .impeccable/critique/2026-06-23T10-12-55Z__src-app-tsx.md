#### Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment**: This does not fail as unusable AI slop, but it does read as a polished prompt-generated dashboard. The repeated elevated translucent panels, oversized hero, ubiquitous uppercase eyebrows, KPI-card grid, and gradient risk cards pull it toward generic SaaS/fintech demo instead of the “Review Ledger” direction. The strongest product-authored part is the transaction detail panel: it has evidence, recommendation, notes, and governance in one place.

**Deterministic scan**: CLI scan of `src\App.tsx` returned clean (`[]`). Browser overlay scan found **10 anti-pattern signals**: `hero-eyebrow-chip`, `line-length`, two clear low-contrast issues on warning/danger KPI detail text, four near-threshold detail-label contrast warnings, `side-tab` on the governance copy, and `overused-font` / `single-font`. The single-font findings are false positives for this product because PRODUCT/DESIGN explicitly require one sans family. The low-contrast and side-tab findings are valid. The hero eyebrow finding agrees with the design review.

**Visual overlays**: Browser injection succeeded in the **[Human]** tab, and the console reported the findings above. The app also reports a missing `favicon.ico`, which is incidental but worth fixing later.

#### Overall Impression

The dashboard has the right information architecture: overview signals, filters, queue, and detail are all present and broadly understandable. The biggest opportunity is to make it feel less like a generic executive dashboard and more like an accountable review workbench: denser, calmer, more keyboard-accessible, and more explicit about what a reviewer should inspect first.

#### What's Working

- The product purpose is legible: human review, no automated decisioning, rationale, notes, and recommended action are all present.
- The IA foundation is strong: users can move from portfolio-level KPIs to row selection to transaction-level evidence without changing screens.
- Familiar controls are a good product choice: native selects, table rows, pills, and a sticky detail panel fit operational review better than novelty widgets.

#### Priority Issues

**[P1] Keyboard accessibility and focus are not production-ready**

**Why it matters**: The table row is clickable with `onClick`, but only the transaction ID button is keyboard-focusable. There are no explicit focus-visible styles for buttons, selects, textarea, or selected rows. A keyboard-only reviewer can technically use the ID buttons, but the intended row-selection model is visually and interactively inconsistent.

**Fix**: Make row selection a single accessible pattern: either turn the row’s primary cell into the only interactive control with full-width hit area, or make rows keyboard-focusable with `role`, `tabIndex`, Enter/Space handling, and visible focus. Add `:focus-visible` styles for `.ghost-button`, `.row-button`, `select`, and `textarea`.

**Suggested command**: `/impeccable audit src\App.tsx`

**[P1] The visual system drifts into generic glass dashboard language**

**Why it matters**: PRODUCT.md says calm, precise, accountable and explicitly avoids decorative glassy KPI cards and command-center polish. Current panels, KPI cards, and governance card all share translucent backgrounds, blur, and large shadows. Combined with tinted gradient KPI backgrounds, it weakens the evidence-first ledger feel.

**Fix**: Keep major surfaces white/opaque or lightly tonal, reduce blur, lower shadow intensity, remove KPI gradients, and use status chips or small semantic markers for risk instead of whole-card decorative color fields.

**Suggested command**: `/impeccable quieter src\styles.css`

**[P1] Repeated eyebrows and oversized hero make the page feel promotional**

**Why it matters**: Product users need to enter triage quickly. The hero consumes too much attention, and every panel repeats the tiny tracked uppercase label pattern. This is one of the strongest “AI made that” tells and conflicts with DESIGN.md’s Label Scarcity Rule.

**Fix**: Reduce the hero to a compact operational header, keep one clear product title, and replace most eyebrows with useful metadata: result counts, data freshness, SLA scope, or filter state.

**Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout src\App.tsx`

**[P2] Triage prioritization is present but not decisive**

**Why it matters**: A reviewer sees KPIs, trend, five filters, table, risk badges, SLA labels, and detail all at once. The interface does not clearly answer “open this first.” High-risk and SLA-breached items are counted, but not turned into a prioritized worklist affordance.

**Fix**: Add an explicit priority sort/default ordering, strengthen the selected/highest-priority row affordance, and consider a compact “Needs attention” summary that names the top reason: overdue SLA, critical risk, or exposure.

**Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden src\App.tsx`

**[P2] Recommendation copy risks implying system authority**

**Why it matters**: “Recommended next action” is helpful, but in this domain it can sound like the system is deciding. PRODUCT.md requires human review to remain the center of gravity.

**Fix**: Reframe as reviewer support: “Suggested reviewer next step” or “Review prompt,” then pair it with “Reviewer owns outcome” adjacent to the action, not only at the bottom of the panel.

**Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify src\App.tsx`

#### Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User)**: Alex can scan the table, but cannot sort by SLA, risk, exposure, or status; cannot save a filter view; cannot bulk-review; and gets no keyboard-first row navigation. The decorative hero and repeated eyebrows slow the path to the queue.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)**: Sam gets native select controls, but focus styling is effectively absent. The intended clickable-row behavior is not announced as a single keyboard-operable control. Low-contrast text appears on the warning/danger KPI detail backgrounds, and the trend chart needs a text summary for non-visual interpretation.

**Mina (Financial Crime Reviewer / Compliance Lead)**: Mina needs audit confidence and a clear first case to inspect. The dashboard shows rationale, SLA, and governance, but the top-priority case is not explicitly called out. The “recommended next action” language needs to stay visibly subordinate to human judgment.

#### Minor Observations

- The `border-left: 4px` governance callout violates the side-stripe ban; replace with a full-border note, icon/label treatment, or tonal notice.
- The table’s `min-width: 900px` keeps desktop density but can hide SLA/status on smaller screens; mobile needs a deliberate review-card or priority-column pattern.
- The trend chart lacks a textual summary such as “10 flagged over 7 days; peak day Jun 21.”
- The missing favicon produces a browser console 404.
- Detail label contrast is right at the threshold; bump `#60748b` darker on `#f3f7fb`.

#### Questions to Consider

- What would this look like if it were designed like an audit ledger, not a dashboard demo?
- Which single signal should tell a reviewer “open this first”?
- Can every visual treatment justify itself as evidence, orientation, or accountability?
